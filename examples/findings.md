# Findings: Regional support performance by cohort

> **This is a worked example built entirely from synthetic data.** No real company,
> customer, or vendor is represented here. The dataset is generated by
> `analysis/queries/01_make_synthetic_source.py` with a fixed seed, and every number
> below is produced by `analysis/compute.py` — run `python3 analysis/compute.py --validate`
> to reproduce them.

## Dataset Summary
- Source: `companies.csv` (synthetic — 480 fictional B2B SaaS companies)
- Time period: 2022–2025 annual cohorts
- Sample size: 480 companies (30 per region-cohort cell, 4 regions × 4 cohorts)
- Tables or files queried: `analysis/results/companies.csv`

## Headline Findings

1. **Median ticket resolution in the West improved 33.2% across cohorts — from 12.3h in the 2022 cohort to 8.2h in 2025 — while the Northeast degraded 35.8% over the same window (6.6h to 8.9h).**

2. **The gap between the fastest and slowest region collapsed 82.5%, from 5.73 hours in the 2022 cohort to 1.00 hour in 2025.** Four regions that were clearly differentiated four years ago are now effectively indistinguishable on this metric.

3. **Resolution time and satisfaction correlate at r = −0.694 (R² = 0.482).** Each additional hour of resolution time costs roughly 0.131 CSAT points. Resolution speed explains a little under half the variance in satisfaction — meaningful, but not the whole story.

4. **The spread within every region is wider than the gap between regions.** The narrowest within-region spread (Midwest, 4.99h from P10 to P90) is more than double the entire between-region gap (2.35h). Knowing a company's region tells you very little about its actual resolution time.

## Anomalies & Surprises

- **The best-performing region is the only one getting materially worse.** The Northeast started as the clear leader (6.6h, 1.5h faster than the next-best region) and is the only region whose trajectory degraded by more than 3%. Southeast (−3.0%) and Midwest (0.0%) were essentially flat.
- **Convergence came from both directions at once.** It would be unremarkable if laggards simply caught up. Here the leader regressed toward the pack at nearly the same rate the laggard advanced — 35.8% up against 33.2% down.
- **The West still has the widest internal spread** (7.10h P10–P90) despite posting the largest improvement, suggesting its gains are unevenly distributed rather than broad-based.

## Narrative Angles

1. **Convergence** — The laggard is improving at roughly the same rate the leader is declining, and the category is flattening into sameness. If resolution speed was ever a regional advantage, it has a four-year half-life. Draws on findings 1 and 2 plus the first anomaly.

2. **Within beats between** — Picking a region tells you almost nothing; picking a company tells you everything. The between-group comparison that structures most reporting on this metric is the least informative cut available. Draws on finding 4.

3. **Speed is necessary but not sufficient** — Resolution time explains 48% of satisfaction variance, which means a majority sits elsewhere. Teams optimizing resolution time alone are tuning half the instrument. Draws on finding 3.
